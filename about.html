We plan in two languages. The first is ours — the particular hush of a mandap minutes before the bride arrives, the smell of marigold strung overnight, the grandmother who must be seated exactly there. The second is the language your photographer's editor speaks in Paris: light, negative space, restraint.

The two should not compete. A Humsafar wedding is the proof that they can agree — that a celebration can be unmistakably Indian and carry itself like an international design publication, and that the two registers make each other look better.

We keep three desks — planning, decoration, entertainment — each run by a founder, each accountable to the others. Nothing is outsourced to a stranger. Every wedding is held by people who will be in the room.
